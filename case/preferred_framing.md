# Preferred Framing

This file guides how legal-bot frames issues in review outputs, attorney handoffs, and suggested edits. The goal is not timid language. The goal is framing that is source-backed, strategically purposeful, court-credible, and tied to relief.

## Framing Is Context-Specific

The right frame depends on:

- document type
- audience
- relief requested
- source support
- legal authority status
- child impact
- attorney strategy
- procedural posture

Measured language does not mean bland language. It means the framing is specific, grounded, and useful.

## Core Family-Law Frames

1. Child best interests
2. Stability and continuity
3. Status quo preservation
4. Joint legal custody and shared decision-making process
5. Notice, consent, and transparency
6. Order compliance
7. Practical co-parenting function
8. Source-backed chronology
9. Professional and provider support
10. Administrable relief
11. Narrow relief when it is stronger than broad relief
12. Accountability without personal attack

## Status Quo / Turning Point Framing

Legal-bot should identify when a filing should emphasize preserving, restoring, or preventing disruption of the status quo.

Examples:

- school placement
- parent-time schedule
- therapy or provider arrangements
- medical decision-making process
- custody evaluation posture
- existing court orders
- established division of duties

When status quo matters, the framing should state:

- what the status quo is
- how it was established
- what event disrupted or threatens it
- why the court should care
- what relief preserves, restores, or clarifies it

## Strong but Credible Advocacy

Strong advocacy is appropriate when it makes the court's decision easier and is supported by facts.

Strong advocacy should:

- connect facts to relief
- identify harm without exaggeration
- explain urgency
- preserve credibility
- avoid unsupported motive claims
- use dates and documents
- respect attorney strategy

Measured language does not mean timid language. It means source-backed, specific, strategically purposeful, court-credible, and tied to relief.

## Court-Facing vs Attorney-Facing vs Internal Strategy

Use these buckets consistently:

- `court_safe_candidate`: filing-safe, precise, evidence-grounded, and relief-oriented
- `attorney_safe`: candid enough for attorney review, but not necessarily appropriate to file
- `internal_only`: private strategy, blunt risk analysis, theory-testing, and option generation

Internal-only strategy may be direct or skeptical, but it should not leak into declarations, motions, proposed orders, or co-parenting communications.

## Framing by Document Type

### Motion to Enforce
- goal: show a clear obligation, a documented failure to comply, and relief the court can administer
- tone: firm, specific, compliance-focused
- common risks: vague accusations, missing order cites, asking for relief broader than the violation supports
- good framing: "paragraph [X] required [duty]; on [date(s)] the record shows [noncompliance]"
- avoid or soften: generalized grievance language untethered to an order provision

### TRO / Emergency Motion
- goal: explain immediate risk, why ordinary timing is inadequate, and what narrow relief is needed now
- tone: urgent, controlled, credible
- common risks: overstatement, conclusory fear language, skipping chronology
- good framing: specific incidents, specific timing, concrete harm, practical relief
- avoid or soften: rhetoric that outruns the proof

### Opposition / Response
- goal: narrow the dispute, expose gaps, and make the requested denial or narrowing easy
- tone: pointed, organized, confident
- common risks: mirroring the other side's heat, over-defensiveness, arguing every small point
- good framing: "the record does not support [claim]; the stronger view is [supported position]"
- avoid or soften: unnecessary moral framing

### Reply
- goal: answer the point that matters most and restore the clean line from record to relief
- tone: concise, corrective, strategic
- common risks: repeating the opening motion, expanding the dispute, sounding irritated
- good framing: short chronology corrections, relief alignment, targeted rebuttal
- avoid or soften: side fights that do not move the decision

### Declaration
- goal: provide admissible-style factual support, chronology, and firsthand observations
- tone: factual, careful, direct
- common risks: motive claims, legal argument disguised as fact, private strategy leakage
- good framing: what happened, when, who observed it, what source confirms it
- avoid or soften: diagnosis, editorializing, and legal conclusions

### Proposed Order
- goal: state exactly what must happen, by whom, by when, and how disagreements are handled
- tone: neutral, precise, enforceable
- common risks: argumentative language, vague obligations, missing deadlines
- good framing: operational language and defined mechanisms
- avoid or soften: advocacy, narrative, and surplus adjectives

### Attorney Handoff Email
- goal: summarize the real problem, strongest support, open authority questions, and best options
- tone: direct, practical, high-signal
- common risks: overloading counsel with low-value issues
- good framing: prioritized bullets, risk tags, and narrow asks
- avoid or soften: performative certainty when research or facts remain open

### Co-Parenting Communication
- goal: solve the immediate logistics problem while protecting the record
- tone: restrained, child-focused, non-inflammatory
- common risks: bait-taking, argument by text, moral accusations
- good framing: short factual requests, clear deadlines, calm confirmations
- avoid or soften: sarcasm, legal threats, and blame language

### Mediation / Settlement Document
- goal: show practical options and leverage without poisoning the process
- tone: firm, solution-oriented, reality-based
- common risks: overclaiming, unnecessary escalation, hidden strategy leakage
- good framing: operational proposals, defined tradeoffs, documented concerns
- avoid or soften: all-or-nothing posturing unless counsel wants that posture

## Framing by Issue

### Custody and Parent-Time
- frame around: continuity, developmental needs, functioning schedule, child impact, and workable transitions
- good framing: stability, school-week rhythm, exchange reliability, child response to routine
- avoid or soften: ownership language, score-settling, "winning custody"

### Medical / Mental Health Decisions
- frame around: notice, records, provider input, consent process, and child impact
- good framing: who knew what, when notice was given, what provider guidance exists
- avoid or soften: unsupported claims about diagnosis, intent, or danger

### Therapy / Provider Decisions
- frame around: continuity of care, provider recommendations, participation, and documented concerns
- good framing: referral history, provider notes, scheduling obstacles, decision path
- avoid or soften: professional-label claims unsupported by records

### School Decisions
- frame around: continuity, logistics, notice, educational impact, and decision-making authority
- good framing: unilateral changes, transportation burdens, attendance, provider or teacher input
- avoid or soften: identity-based attacks instead of school-function facts

### Communication / OFW
- frame around: what was communicated, what was not, timing, and practical impact
- good framing: missed notice, refusal to answer a narrow question, escalation pattern
- avoid or soften: attacks on tone untethered to practical effect

### Financial Support
- frame around: documented obligations, payment history, missing records, and effect on children or case administration
- good framing: dates, amounts, logs, declarations, reimbursements, undisclosed gaps
- avoid or soften: speculation about hidden money without records

### Fees / Sanctions
- frame around: documented conduct, unnecessary motion practice, noncompliance costs, and why narrower remedies were inadequate
- good framing: chronology of avoidable expense and the specific record that caused it
- avoid or soften: punitive rhetoric unless counsel is intentionally making that argument

### TRO / Safety Concerns
- frame around: specific conduct, specific risk, timing, and the narrowest effective protection
- good framing: incidents, witnesses, records, escalating sequence, child impact if applicable
- avoid or soften: broad labels without concrete acts

### GAL / Evaluator / Special Master
- frame around: what problem needs a neutral professional process and why the existing process is failing
- good framing: repeated decision deadlock, child-function concerns, provider conflict, implementation breakdown
- avoid or soften: implying support from a professional who has not said so

### Contempt / Enforcement
- frame around: obligation, notice, ability to comply if known, and documented failure
- good framing: exact order provision, dates, proof of notice, and resulting problem
- avoid or soften: conclusory contempt language without legal fit or authority

### Expert or Evaluation Strategy
- frame around: what question needs neutral expertise, what record is missing, and how the expert could change posture
- good framing: clear gap in child, financial, vocational, or mental health evidence
- avoid or soften: expert-shopping language or pre-judging the outcome
